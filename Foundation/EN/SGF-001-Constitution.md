## I. Preamble

ShadowGuard Initiative was born from a simple idea: help people use the Internet more safely.

It did not begin with a business plan, investment capital, or a chase after trends.  
It began with genuine care for a person who matters.

The first project of the initiative is **Shadvert** — a tool for checking ads, links, and suspicious offers, explained in everyday language.

This Constitution states **who we are, whom we serve, and how we decide**.  
It is not a marketing brochure. It is a commitment to the people who place their trust in us.

---

## II. Mission

Our goal is **not to scare people**.

Our goal is to give them **confidence** and help them make **safer decisions**.

We want to reduce confusion and fear of technology — not inflate them.  
We want a person to understand *why* something looks fine or suspicious, and *what to do next*.

---

## III. Whom we help

Anyone who has ever asked:

> “Can I trust this?”

Especially people who:

- have no technical training and no desire to learn jargon,
- get lost easily in modern apps,
- need a clear second opinion before they click, pay, or believe someone,
- want to help parents and grandparents stay safer online.

Family is a natural model: the person who knows more tech can set things up and watch over them; the person who needs calm gets a simple path.

---

## IV. Four pillars

Everything created under ShadowGuard Initiative must stand on these values — as practice, not empty slogans.

### 1. Simplicity

Simplicity is not a feature. It is a commitment.

- Every screen should be understandable within a few seconds.
- Every message should use everyday language.
- Complexity may exist inside the system, but it **must not burden the user**.

Simplicity is not a lack of capability.  
It is a **hierarchy of attention**: essentials first; the rest only when someone wants more.

### 2. Reliability

Honest uncertainty beats false calm.

- A verdict should be clear and consistent.
- When the system is unsure, it must say so plainly.
- When a service fails (network, AI, server), the user gets a plain explanation — not a blank screen and fear.

### 3. Security

**Security builds trust.**

- Protecting keys, data, and access is not a task to postpone.
- Sensitive data does not belong in the frontend, in git history, or in unnecessary logs.
- The system should resist abuse (including overload and API theft) in proportion to its scale.

Without security, talk of trust is empty.

### 4. Trust

Trust cannot be bought with slogans. It is earned through explanation and consistency.

- Every recommendation must be **explainable**.
- We are not a magic black box.
- We are not a legal guarantee or a police verdict — we are a **helper toward better decisions**. The final choice remains with the person.

---

## V. Transparency

Every recommendation must be explainable.

The user should receive:

1. a clear verdict (for example: looks fine / be careful / suspicious),
2. concrete reasons in plain language,
3. guidance on **what to do now**.

Where evidence is missing, false certainty must not appear.

---

## VI. Technology

**AI is a tool, not the goal.**

- Artificial intelligence helps explain and complement — it does not decide blindly or absolutely.
- Known scam patterns and verifiable facts (for example domain age, blocklists, technical checks) take priority where they provide a solid base.
- Hallucinations and invented “facts” are unacceptable as the sole basis of a verdict for vulnerable users.

Technology serves people. Not the other way around.

---

## VII. Rule for new features

Every new feature must do at least one of the following:

- save time,
- reduce stress,
- increase safety.

And it must also pass the founding question (Article IX).

A feature that only “looks smart” but confuses, burdens, or weakens trust does not belong in the product — even if it is technically interesting.

---

## VIII. Future and ecosystem

ShadowGuard Initiative is a long-term commitment.

**Shadvert** is the first chapter — not the whole book.

Further applications in the same spirit may come only after the first chapter holds up in real life: clear, reliable, secure, and trusted by the people who use it.

An ecosystem without a solid core is only a pile of half-finished pieces.  
Therefore: **quality of one thing first, breadth later.**

The name people see on the first product is **Shadvert**.  
**ShadowGuard Initiative** is the overarching commitment in the background.

---

## IX. Founding question

Every line of code, every document, and every decision under ShadowGuard Initiative should stand up to one question:

> **Will this help someone feel safer online?**

If the answer is yes — we continue.  
If not — we stop and look for a better solution.

---

## X. Mission motto

We do not want people to fear the Internet.

We want them to understand it well enough to make safer decisions.
