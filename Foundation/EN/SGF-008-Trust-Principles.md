**Motto:** Trust cannot be bought with advertising. It is earned through behaviour.

---

## I. Purpose

The **Constitution** names trust as one of four pillars.  
The **Manifesto** says technology earns respect through capability and **trust through how it behaves**.

**Trust Principles** turn trust into **concrete commitments** to people who give us a chance — especially non-technical users and families.

This is not a legal contract.  
It is a promise for how the product should behave even when no one is watching.

---

## II. What trust means here

Trust is not “the user believes us blindly”.

Trust is:

1. **I understand** what is happening,  
2. **I know** why you are telling me this,  
3. **I feel** you are not scaring or cheating me,  
4. **I can** verify or leave without punishment.

If any of that is missing, we do not have trust — we only have traffic.

---

## III. Ten trust principles

### 1. Transparency before magic

Every important verdict and recommendation must be **explainable** in everyday language.

- Not only a traffic-light colour.  
- Reasons.  
- What the person should do now.

A black box of “just trust the AI” does not belong in ShadowGuard.

### 2. Explain before warning

Explain first, then warn (see PRINCIPLES.md).

- Panic without reason destroys trust as much as false calm.  
- A warning must have content: *why* and *what to do*.  
- Fear for “engagement” is forbidden.

### 3. Honest uncertainty before false certainty

When we are unsure, **we say so**.

- Yellow / “we don’t know enough” is a valid result.  
- A false green for a vulnerable user is worse than no tool.  
- A false red that scares people for no reason also erodes trust.

### 4. Evidence before guesswork

Order of judgement:

**verifiable signal → logical pattern → AI support → impression**

- AI must not be the only source of a “fact” (domain age, company existence, official status).  
- Hybrid rules take priority for known patterns.  
- A guess may appear only as a guess — not as certainty.

### 5. Privacy by default

- We collect **only what is needed** for the function, security, or a family model the user knowingly enabled.  
- No unnecessary profiles “for marketing”.  
- Sensitive content (SMS text, links, screenshots) is not used to train external models beyond the necessary API call and the provider’s terms — and even then we choose the minimum.  
- Logs must not become a warehouse of personal data “just in case”.

### 6. Minimum data, maximum usefulness

Every stored piece of data must pass:

> *Do we need this so someone is safer or calmer — or are we only hoarding?*

If we do not need it — **we do not store it**.  
If we store it — we know **why**, **how long**, and **who may see it** (e.g. family sync).

### 7. Security without fear

Security builds trust — but the **tone** must not be horror.

- Keys, tokens, rate limits, no secrets in the frontend: mandatory.  
- We speak plainly to the user, not in exploit jargon.  
- “Security without fear” — protection yes, panic no.

### 8. No absolute promise

We are not police, court, or insurance.

- We provide **recommendations and explanations**, not a legal verdict.  
- The final choice remains with the person.  
- The disclaimer is not fine print — it is part of honesty.

### 9. Consistency over time

Trust breaks when product behaviour changes without reason or explanation.

- Changes that affect verdicts or privacy should be understandable.  
- Family members who help set things up must not be blindsided by silent rule changes.  
- Mistakes are admitted and fixed — not papered over.

### 10. Trust before features

A new feature that reduces clarity, privacy, or honesty of the verdict **does not ship** — even if it is technically interesting.

> Never sacrifice trust for speed.

---

## IV. Trust in the family model

Shadvert assumes that often **someone in the family** sets it up and **someone less technical** uses it.

Commitments:

- Family linking only with a **conscious code / consent**, not in secret.  
- History and alerts serve protection, not control for its own sake.  
- The helper gets a clear overview; the user must not be overwhelmed.  
- No hidden third-party tracking without a clear reason and explanation.

---

## V. Trust and AI

| May | Must not |
|-----|----------|
| Explain suspicion in human language | Invent facts |
| Suggest a next step | Claim absolute certainty without basis |
| Complement hybrid rules | Be the sole judge on a critical verdict |
| Fail understandably (fallback) | Fail silently with a blank screen |

When AI is unavailable, the user gets a **clear message** and, where possible, a **rule-based fallback** — not silence.

---

## VI. Short trust checklist (before “done”)

- [ ] Can a non-IT person understand it in a few seconds?  
- [ ] Are the reasons for the verdict clear?  
- [ ] Do we admit uncertainty where it exists?  
- [ ] Are we storing unnecessary data?  
- [ ] Can keys or sensitive data leak?  
- [ ] Is the tone needlessly scary?  
- [ ] Are we promising more than we can defend?  
- [ ] Will this help someone feel **safer and calmer** online?

If you cannot answer one of these — **it is not trustworthy enough yet**.

## VIII. Closing

We will not earn trust with a logo or a slogan.

We earn it by:

- telling the truth even when it is uncomfortable,  
- keeping data in check,  
- explaining instead of scaring,  
- and never sacrificing the calm of the person on the other side of the screen just to look “smarter” or “faster”.
